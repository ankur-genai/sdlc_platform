SECTION 2 — BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT  (JSON key: "detailed_brd")
=====================================================================
Produce a FULL Business Requirements Document as a single markdown-formatted
string. It must read like a real BRD a client sponsor would review and sign off
on — not a paragraph, not an outline. Use `##`/`###` headings, markdown tables,
and bullet lists inside the string, and escape newlines for JSON.

Include, at minimum, every section below, in this order:

## 1. Executive Summary
   A crisp overview of the initiative, the business context, and the expected
   outcome — understandable by a non-technical executive in under two minutes.

## 2. Business Problem / Opportunity
   The concrete pain, cost, risk, or market opportunity being addressed. Quantify
   where the requirements allow (time lost, error rates, revenue at stake).

## 3. Business Objectives & Goals
   SMART objectives (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound).
   Present as a bullet list or table; each objective should be measurable and
   traceable to a success metric in Section 10.

## 4. Project Scope
   ### In Scope  — bullet list of what WILL be delivered.
   ### Out of Scope — bullet list of what will explicitly NOT be delivered
   (guarding against scope creep).

## 5. Stakeholders
   A markdown table with columns: Stakeholder / Role | Interest & Influence |
   Key Concerns. Include sponsors, process owners, end users, and any
   regulator/compliance stakeholders implied by the domain.

## 6. Business Requirements (grouped by theme/capability)
   Number each business requirement with a stable ID (BR-001, BR-002 …) and map
   each to the epics it drives. Use a table: BR ID | Requirement | Priority
   (MoSCoW) | Related Epic(s). These are business-level "what/why" requirements,
   distinct from the system-level "how" requirements in the SRS.

## 7. Assumptions
   Material assumptions made where the input was silent — each one explicit, so
   stakeholders can confirm or correct it.

## 8. Constraints
   Business, regulatory, budget, timeline, technology, or resourcing constraints
   that bound the solution.

## 9. Dependencies
   External systems, teams, vendors, data sources, or upstream/downstream
   initiatives this delivery depends on.

Consistency: BR IDs referenced here must be reused in the SRS traceability
tables. Terminology (entities, actors, process names) must match the rest of the
package.
