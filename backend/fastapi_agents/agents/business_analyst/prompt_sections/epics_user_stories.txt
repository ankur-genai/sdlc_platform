SECTION 1 — EPICS + USER STORIES  (JSON key: "epics")
=====================================================
Group the approved requirements into logical, cohesive EPICS. An epic is a
significant, independently-valuable body of work (e.g. "User Authentication",
"Account Management", "Payments & Transfers"). Aim for 3–8 epics for a typical
system; do not force everything into one epic, and do not fragment into trivial
epics.

Each epic object contains:
- "title": short, business-meaningful epic name (Title Case).
- "description": 1–2 sentences describing the business capability the epic
  delivers and the value it creates.
- "stories": the list of user stories that realise the epic.

Each USER STORY must be genuinely implementation-ready:
- "id": sequential story identifier, ZERO-PADDED to three digits, starting at
  US-001 and incrementing across the WHOLE package (US-001, US-002, US-003 …),
  never restarting per epic and never skipping numbers.
- "epic": the exact "title" of the epic this story belongs to (must match).
- "title": a concise action-oriented story title.
- "role": the persona/actor (the "As a ___" part) — reuse persona names where
  possible for traceability.
- "goal": what they want to do (the "I want ___" part), phrased as a capability.
- "benefit": why it matters (the "So that ___" part) — a real business outcome,
  not a restatement of the goal.
- "acceptance_criteria": 2–4 criteria, EACH in Given/When/Then form and EACH
  specific and testable. Cover the happy path AND at least one edge/negative
  case per story. Never write vague criteria like "should work correctly" or
  "handles errors properly" — state the exact precondition, action, and the
  exact observable outcome (messages, redirects, state changes, values).
- "moscow": MoSCoW priority — EXACTLY one of: Must, Should, Could, Won't.
- "points": relative complexity estimate on a Fibonacci scale — an INTEGER from
  {1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13}. Reserve 8/13 for genuinely complex or high-uncertainty
  work; keep simple CRUD/display stories at 1–3. Estimates should reflect real
  relative complexity, not be uniform.

Quality checks before you finish this section:
- Every requirement in the input is covered by at least one story.
- Every epic has at least one "Must" story (or a documented reason it does not).
- Story IDs are contiguous and unique across the whole package.
